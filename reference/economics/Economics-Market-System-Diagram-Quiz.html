1. PPC — movement M → N

📄 Cambridge IGCSE Econ 0455/11 (2024) Q3MCQ

The diagram shows a production possibility curve (PPC) for an economy. What is the reason for the economy moving from point M to point N?

A. decreased unemployment in industries producing good X
B. improvement in technology used to produce good X
C. increased productivity of workers producing good X
D. increased resource allocation to the production of good X

✅ Answer: D

  • Both M and N lie on the PPC — so the economy is already fully efficient at each point. The curve itself hasn't moved.
  • Moving along the curve from M to N means producing more of good X and less of good Y (N is further along the X axis).
  • That is a reallocation of resources: more resources were shifted into good X production.
  • D — increased resource allocation to good X — is correct.
  • A, B and C are wrong because they would shift the whole PPC outward (better technology, higher productivity, more employed workers) or instead move a point toward the curve from inside — none of which is what happens when you slide along an existing curve.

2. Supply curve — shift S₁ → S₂

📄 Cambridge IGCSE Econ 0455/11 (2024) Q5MCQ

The diagram shows a shift in the supply curve for New Zealand's airlines from S₁ to S₂. What is the most likely cause of this shift?

A. a decrease in the costs of New Zealand's airlines
B. a decrease in the number of people wanting to fly to New Zealand
C. an increase in the price of train and bus travel in New Zealand
D. an increase in the tax on air travel in New Zealand

✅ Answer: A

  • The shift is to the right (S₁ → S₂): at every price, airlines are now willing to supply more.
  • A — a decrease in costs — makes flying cheaper to produce, so supply increases. Correct.
  • B is a demand-side change (fewer people wanting to fly) — it shifts demand, not supply.
  • C would raise demand for substitutes… actually bed-time: an increase in the price of train/bus travel makes flying relatively cheaper, raising the demand for flights, not the supply.
  • D — an increase in tax — raises costs and would shift the supply curve to the left, not right.

3. PPC — movement for a policy change

📄 Cambridge IGCSE Econ 0455/11 (2025) Q3MCQ

The diagram shows a production possibility curve (PPC). The economy is using all of its available resources. A government decides to encourage more production of manufactured goods instead of agricultural goods. Which movement shows this policy change?

A. W to X
B. W to Y
C. X to W
D. Z to X

✅ Answer: C

  • The policy wants more manufactured goods, so the economy must move toward the manufactured-goods axis (up/right) on the PPC.
  • C — X to W — moves from a more-agricultural point to a more-manufactured point, all along the curve. Correct.
  • A (W to X) is the opposite — it moves away from manufactured goods.
  • B (W to Y): check the diagram — Y is a point that does not sit further along the manufactured goods axis the policy wants to expand.
  • D (Z to X): similarly, this doesn't end at the more-manufactured point the policy targets.
  • Tip: always identify which axis is 'more manufactured' first (check the point positions on the diagram in the paper), then find the movement that ends further along that axis — that's X to W, answer C.

4. Subsidy shown on a D/S diagram

📄 Cambridge IGCSE Econ 0455/11 (2025) Q6MCQ

The diagram shows demand and supply curves for a product at its equilibrium price P. How would the introduction of a subsidy be shown?

A. Demand would shift to D₁
B. Demand would shift to D₂
C. Supply would shift to S₁
D. Supply would shift to S₂

✅ Answer: D

  • A subsidy is a government payment to producers, which lowers their costs.
  • Lower costs → at every price producers supply more → the supply curve shifts right.
  • D — Supply would shift to S₂ — where S₂ is the rightward curve. Correct.
  • A and B shift demand — but a subsidy changes the supply side, never demand.
  • C (shift to S₁) would be the result of an increase in costs / a tax — the opposite of a subsidy.
  • Memorise: subsidy → supply shifts RIGHT; tax → supply shifts LEFT.

5. (Draw it) Raw-material costs rise — market for cars

📄 Edexcel IGCSE Econ 4EC1 Paper 1 (2025) Q1(g) — 3 marksDrawing — Supply shifts LEFT, price RISES, quantity FALLS

Using the diagram, draw the likely effect on the market for cars following a rise in the costs of raw materials. Label the new curve, the new equilibrium price and the new equilibrium quantity.

✅ Answer: Supply shifts LEFT, price RISES, quantity FALLS

  • What to draw (3 marks, one each):
  • ① Supply curve shifts to the LEFT, labelled (e.g. S₁ → S₂). Raw materials are an input; when they get more expensive, it costs more to make cars, so less is supplied at every price.
  • ② New equilibrium price is HIGHER, labelled (Pe rises). Less supply + same demand pushes the price up.
  • ③ New equilibrium quantity is LOWER, labelled (Qe falls). At the new higher price, fewer cars are bought and sold.
  • Demand does not move — nothing about buyers changed.
  • Mark-scheme pattern for 'cost rise' questions: cost ↑ → supply LEFT → P ↑, Q ↓.

6. PPC — which point is unobtainable?

📄 Edexcel IGCSE Econ 4EC1 Paper 1 (2025) Q2(a) — 1 markMCQ

A country is able to produce agricultural and non-agricultural goods with a given amount of resources. Its production possibility curve (PPC) is shown in Figure 2. Which one of the following points shows unobtainable production?

A. W
B. X
C. Y
D. Z

✅ Answer: C — Y

  • Unobtainable means a mix of goods the economy cannot produce with its current resources and technology — i.e. a point outside (beyond) the PPC.
  • C — Y — Y lies outside the curve, so it is unobtainable. Correct.
  • A (W) is wrong — it lies on the curve, so it's attainable and efficient (all resources fully employed).
  • B (X) is wrong — it lies inside/on the curve, attainable (possibly with some unemployed resources if inside).
  • D (Z) is wrong — it's on an axis, meaning all resources go to one good, fully attainable.
  • Cheat: on the curve = attainable & efficient; inside = inefficient/unemployed; outside = unobtainable.

🧠 Two rules that crack every diagram question

  • Price moves ALONG the line. Nothing-but-price moves THE line (demand/supply shifts only from non-price causes).
  • Supply shifters: costs, technology, tax, subsidy, weather. Tax → supply LEFT; subsidy → supply RIGHT.
  • Cost rises → supply LEFT → P up, Q down. (That's the cars question.)
  • PPC: on = efficient, inside = inefficient/unemployed, outside = unobtainable. Moving along it = reallocating resources.
