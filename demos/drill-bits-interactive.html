📋 HOW TO DRILL A HOLE — step by step

▶
  1. Mark the spot. Pencil cross on wood, then centre-punch it on metal so the bit can't skid.
  2. Choose the bit. Right type for the material, right size, and check it's sharp.
  3. Clamp the work. A bit can snatch and spin the whole piece — always secure it.
  4. Pilot first. For anything over ~6 mm, drill a small pilot hole first.
  5. Start slow and square. Bring the bit to the mark at low speed, then straighten up.
  6. Feed steadily. Let the bit cut; withdraw to clear chips; use fluid on metal.
  7. Ease off at breakthrough. The last few millimetres grab — lighten pressure.
  8. Finish. Deburr the hole, blow out chips, wipe the work.

⚒ SHARPENING & CARE

▶

Twist drills

  • Sharpen on a bench grinder to a 118° point — keep both cutting edges the same length and angle.
  • Dip in water often — overheating turns the steel blue and ruins the temper.
  • A sharpening jig keeps the angles identical; freehand takes practice.

Spade & brad point bits

  • Spade: file the flat cutting corners flat and square — never the centre point.
  • Brad point: lightly stone the inside of the spurs only.

Carbide & diamond tips

  • Masonry, tile and diamond bits are NOT hand-sharpened — replace the tip or the bit.
  • Keep diamond rims wet while drilling; a dry diamond rim dies fast.

Storage

  • Keep bits in a case or rack — rattling in a drawer blunts the edges.
  • Wipe oily shanks, separate wood bits from metal bits, replace worn chucks early.

🔧 THE DRILL MACHINES

▶
Hand Drill & Brace

Manual, slow, total control. The brace + auger is the traditional way to bore deep timber holes.

Power Drill

Corded or cordless, keyed or keyless chuck, often a 2-speed gearbox. Handles nearly every bit here.

Hammer Drill

Adds rapid light percussion — good for masonry bits up to ~13 mm in brick and block.

Rotary Hammer (SDS)

Pneumatic hammer with SDS shanks — big masonry holes, cores and chiselling.

Pillar / Bench Drill

Fixed vertical spindle, depth stop, adjustable speeds. Square, accurate, repeatable holes — best for metalwork.

Magnetic Drill

Clamps magnetically to steel and drives annular cutters — large, precise holes in beams and plate.

📖 GLOSSARY

▶
Shank — the end gripped by the chuck (round, hex, SDS, Weldon).
Flutes — the grooves that carry waste out of the hole.
Swarf — the waste material a bit removes.
Point angle — the angle at the tip; 118° standard for metal.
Pilot hole — a small starter hole drilled before the final size.
Countersink — a cone recess letting a screw head sit flush.
Counterbore — a flat-bottomed recess for a bolt head.
Chamfer — a cut-away edge or corner, usually 45°.
Deburr — to remove the sharp burrs left around a hole.
Run-out — wobble of a bit in the chuck; causes oversize holes.
Cutting fluid — lubricant/coolant for drilling metal.
HSS — high-speed steel — hard, heat-resistant bit steel.
Carbide (TC) — tungsten carbide, the hard tip material for masonry/tile bits.
SDS — a splined shank system that locks into rotary hammers.
Weldon shank — a flat-sided shank for magnetic-drill chucks.
RPM — revolutions per minute — how fast the bit spins.
Chuck — the jaws that grip the shank.
Arbor — the central spindle a hole saw or core bit mounts on.
Annulus — a ring-shaped cut — what hole saws and core bits make.
Breakthrough — the moment the bit exits the far side — grab city.
Feed — how fast you push the bit into the work.
