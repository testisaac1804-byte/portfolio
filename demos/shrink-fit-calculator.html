How to measure so the number is actually useful
  • Rock the jaws, take the smallest. For an outside measurement the true width is the minimum reading — any tilt measures a diagonal and reads big. Slide the jaws until they just kiss both faces, rock them slightly, watch the number; the low point is the part.
  • Big flat jaws only (the bottom pair), thumb-wheel pressure, zero the caliper first and re-zero every few minutes — a warm caliper drifts.
  • Never measure across a corner — corner-to-corner is the diagonal, 40 → 56.6.
  • Avoid the seam and the first 3 mm. The Z-seam is one extra extrusion wide; the bottom 0.3–0.5 mm is elephant's foot. Both read 0.05–0.2 mm over the real face.
  • Take 3 heights × both directions per axis (top −2 mm, middle, bottom +3 mm). Same face, different points: the spread is your flatness error.
  • Deburr before trusting a number. Three light passes of the face on 400-grit laid flat knocks off the seam bead and the foot flare, gives you a real flat to measure, and is exactly the surface the pocket will see.
  • shrink % = (nominal − measured) ÷ nominal × 100  ·  scale = nominal ÷ measured  ·  caliper repeatability ≈ ±0.02 mm
  • A 40 mm cube is a marginal shrink test: 0.04 mm of jaw tilt is 0.1 %, a third of the number you're chasing. For a real shrink constant use a 100 mm+ part; for this job you only need the envelope.
